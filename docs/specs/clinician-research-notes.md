## Findings

**A1 residual kill chance (Partial).** Residual oxidase production tracks survival. p47phox and most
missense mutations keep more of it than nonsense, frameshift, splice or deletion mutations. The
direction is settled. Our 10% and 25% are game numbers and cannot be sourced.
Sources: Kuhns et al., NEJM 2010 (NIH release below).

**A2 sites and organisms (Answered, mostly).** In the NIH cohort lung infection occurred in 87%,
liver abscess in 32%. Per 100 patient-years: Aspergillus 2.6, severe Staphylococcus 1.44,
Burkholderia 1.06, Serratia 0.98, Nocardia 0.81. Staph, Burkholderia, Nocardia and Serratia are
common in the lung. Lymphadenitis and skin and subcutaneous abscesses are common (single-center
series: pneumonia 38, abscess 32, lymphadenitis 29). Lagoon to Streptococcus and Staph is not
sourced. Sources: Marciano 2015; Infection 2015 series.

**A5 fever (Partial).** The CGD Society guide lists 38 C or above among warning signs. The Primary
Immune Foundation says to see the doctor even for minor infections. No standard threshold found.

**A6 exposures (Partial).** Sourced: mulch, compost, hay, dead leaves, potting soil and houseplants,
marijuana (Aspergillus), ponds and brackish water. The CGD Society clinician guide: avoid barns,
caves, sheds and other dusty or damp places. Live trees: an NIH researcher (quoted by the Primary
Immune Foundation) said there is no data on spores, that a fresh healthy tree is safe if care is
taken, and that the tree-stand water can harbor Burkholderia cepacia, so the patient should not
water it. Scuba: no source found. Patient-side (2026-11-16): clinicians told CGD patients to avoid
caves, scuba diving on a vacation off coral reefs, and live Christmas trees brought indoors.

**A7 medicine and swallowed germs (Open).** No source on prophylaxis helping cells finish an
organism already swallowed. Related and sourced (supports B7): TMP-SMX cut non-fungal infections
from 7.1 to 2.4 per 100 patient-months in autosomal recessive CGD and from 15.8 to 6.9 in X-linked,
with no change in fungal infections. Itraconazole cut fungal infections in a randomized trial.
Itraconazole may shift breakthroughs to rare molds.

**A9 diagnosis age (Answered).** X-linked is diagnosed earlier (about 2.1 to 4.9 years). Autosomal
recessive later (mean about 7.8 to 8.8 years in two cohorts). Those are means, not p47phox
specifically. MENA overall median 20 months.

**A10 first serious lung infection (Partial).** Pneumonia, abscess and lymphadenitis are the most
common serious infections. Supports the picture. The "told as one story" framing is ours.

**A14 Burkholderia (Answered).** Both the cepacia group and Burkholderia pseudomallei are reported
in CGD. B. pseudomallei is endemic in southeast Asia and northern Australia, and travel-acquired
melioidosis is documented (over 200 cases; diagnostic delays up to 18 months). Lung is a common
site. Catalase-positive and covered by TMP-SMX. No large study of melioidosis risk in CGD travelers.

**A15 Salmonella (Partial).** Real in CGD and often invasive. A Colombian cohort of 39 patients
isolated Salmonella from 17 (12 with bacteremia, several deaths from sepsis). A review covers 13
published cases of Salmonella brain abscess in CGD. Not a leading organism in the MENA series. So:
not mainly the lung. The shell question (finish versus contain) and prophylaxis are Open.

**B2 catalase (Partial).** Reviews say most pathogens carry catalase, streptococci are the notable
exception, and catalase status does not fully explain which organisms cause trouble. Matches our
hedging. The wording question is ours.

**B3 medicine names (Partial).** Standard prophylaxis is TMP-SMX twice daily, itraconazole once
daily, and interferon-gamma three times a week. So the question is whether we may leave
interferon-gamma out of the game, not whether it is used.

**B4 more organisms (Answered for five).** Rates above (A2). Granulibacter, Chromobacterium,
Salmonella and A. nidulans were not found.

**B7 medicine and what arrives (Partial).** Supported: prophylaxis lowers infection rates without
removing them (A7).

**B9 after a successful transplant or gene therapy (Partial).** A 391-patient multi-institution
study: durable resolution of CGD symptoms, 3-year survival 82%, graft failure or second transplant
in 17.6%. At 3 to 5 years, less antimicrobial prophylaxis and steroid use than before. No formal
rule for when prophylaxis stops, so that looks center-specific. Patient-side (2026-11-16): in the
few transplant and gene therapy patients Tracy knows, prophylaxis is discontinued, restrictions ease
slowly over time, and the immune response is about normal (DHR at least 20 to 30%).

**B10a 20 to 30% corrected cells (Answered, approximately).** In 162 affected female carriers, a
DHR+ value under 10% was strongly associated with infections (odds ratio 99), and under 20% still
(odds ratio 12). Gene therapy (Kohn) reported DHR+ in more than 15% of neutrophils. Not a defined
threshold. Wording stays hedged. Tracy notes the 20 to 30 and nearly 70 percent figures came from clinicians and a gene therapy patient's own DHR result, so the carrier data is context only.

**B13 Burkholderia and medicine (Partial).** TMP-SMX covers Burkholderia, Serratia, Nocardia and
Staph. Breakthrough infections are a recognised problem but are not quantified in what I found.

**B14 travel (Partial).** Advice: check with the immunology team first, use a travel clinic that
knows immunodeficiency, prefer chlorinated pools to ocean or fresh water, avoid barns, caves and
sheds, bring any interferon supply. Supports the travel plan.

**B16 other settings (Partial).** Potting soil and houseplants are listed as risks, which supports
the flytrap in the house. Green woodworking, crawlspaces and koji: nothing found.

**C2 vaccines (Answered for BCG).** BCG is a live attenuated vaccine and complication rates are
significantly higher in immunodeficient people, CGD named among the highest-risk groups. None of 7
BCG-vaccinated CGD patients in that transplant series had post-transplant complications. No source
on other vaccines. A travel-medicine review says live viral vaccines can be given when the defect is
limited to phagocytes, live bacterial vaccines cannot. Not verified. Patient-side: Tracy has never
been told to avoid any.

**Steroids (not sent, deferred).** Supported for granulomatous colitis and obstruction. Thin for
Aspergillus pneumonia (case reports). Nothing on the immunosuppression trade-off.
